# Playtest fixes

Five hand playtests (`eval-out/playtest-*/NOTES.md`) found what follows. Each batch appends its own heading.

## Playtest fixes (batch 1): stability, orders and the ledger

1. **A local id built from an entity id.** `child:rome-kin-3ualq2:21` and `titus-vettius:comrade:4` failed the local-id rule and killed the whole burst. `sim/safe-local-id.ts` (`safeLocalId`) mends any id (lower case, other characters to underscores, cut to sixty with a hash of the whole) and leaves a valid one alone; every `localId:` template in the sim goes through it. `villainy.ts` vendettas, defamers and rivals are grown (`ADULT_YEARS`, fifteen) and living men only.
2. **The silent freeze.** A four-hundred-character promise went into a two-hundred-character label, and every tick after it was set aside without a word. `sim/clip-overlong.ts` cuts any string that is too long for its field (found from the schema's own `too_big` issues) in the tick, in the held bookkeeping steps and in a batch of acts; the producers in `promises.ts` and `commitments.ts` clip at source. A tick that still will not load stops the burst with stop reason `tick_failed`, puts the clock back to the last sound hop, logs the failing path to the console and tells the player.
3. **The engine does not skim for the player.** `keepDepartments` takes an `exempt` set (the player); monthly diversion skips his hands. He may take from the chest only by an order he writes, and an audit finds that as any other.
4. **A man with no charge builds nothing for the state.** The build template and the competence gate refuse a work paid from the public chest by a man who holds no seat and heads no power, in period words.
5. **The ledger tells what the world shows.** `OrderPart.actsDone` counts carried acts that are their own evidence (`DONE_ON_APPLICATION_OPS`: a purse debited, a holding made, a loan opened, men raised); where all are, the part is done, not "ordered; no sign of it yet". "Ordered, no sign" lapses after sixty days and is listed only for the latest order. A motion is always told (carried with the count, failed, or lapsed). "Nothing came of it that anyone could see" is not said of an order that was carried out. A speak-for order is not "raised" by a force that merely shares a name. A retried orchestrator part replaces its first answer (acts, facts, delegations) instead of standing beside it; a part refused with a reason, or told to stay as he is, is not asked for again; the same order is not handed to the same man twice; one usurpation is one answerable entry.
6. **Refusals give their reason.** The list of what is under way shows the engine's reason for a refused candidacy; a "propose" order refused by the army's indifference is told truly; a man refused by one he asked has that man's reason.
7. **Dispatches** go only to the man who holds the command above the sender (the army's controller) or heads the power; the books are reported to a magistrate who does not keep them; a doubled "writes:" is stripped; numbers are said as a man would (`menInWords`).
8. **A quaestor, a paymaster or any man of the supply** may say "find supply for the consul's army" (`isSupplyHand`, `forcesUnder(..., { forSupply: true })`); he does it himself.
9. **Declared people are made with the player** (`shared/characters/declared-kin.ts`: wife, son, patron, friend, with family links; the web's confirmation finds them there by name), re-enlistment keeps the service record, and a name another person bears gets "the Younger" and so on.
10. **The fact flood.** `isFarRoutine`/`ROUTINE_NEWS_KINDS` in `shared/world/facts.ts`: the calendar of a power that is not the observer's (terms, elections, harvests, census) is not known to him unless he found it out himself. Rome's own calendar stays.
11. **Stale text.** The seed line about a man's purse follows the purse; a soldier of the line with no age declared is seventeen to twenty-five, not thirty-five.
12. **A carried motion that begins nothing** says it is a resolution of the Senate that no one is charged with. Turning the carried text into an act by rule is batch 4's (item 2).

Open decisions: item 12's by-rule execution; the orchestrator retry no longer asks again for a refusal the model gave with a reason (`orchestrate-split.test.ts` follows); whether the player should see the far calendar in a single batched line.

## Playtest fixes (batch 3): elections, the economy, the merchant and the defector

## Defection gaps closed

Batch 3's defector (item 12 above) had three gaps left open: the consul received any man "under his protection" at once, a hired band was drawn up as the employer's legion, and a defection left his bargains and his bands as they were. Each is closed by rule (no model call, no new prompt text).

1. **The Senate's reception decree** (`sim/reception.ts`, called by `receiveDefector`, settled by `keepTheBodies` each day). Rome gave a refugee of weight *hospitium* by decree, the consul laying it before the house (Livy 21.12; 42.19). `rankOf` reads a man *before* his armies are handed on: **a general** (a seat in a general's office, or command of a thousand men), **an officer with men**, or **a man of standing** (any held seat, or standing of 6,000). A humble man is received by the consul's own word as before (patron link, "under his protection"). A man of rank who goes over to a power that keeps a council (`receptionChamber`) is a **suppliant** (status `suppliant`, beside `foreigner` and `deserter`): the receiving consul's motion "that X, a general who came over from Carthage, be received as a guest of Rome and given hospitium" is put to the house (a `council_deliberation`, `subjectKind` character, ten days; the vote is the usual one by the chamber's blocs, and its agenda, quorum and tribunes are the chamber's). What his old power did the day he left (estate, hostages, infamy) stands whatever the vote. **Carried:** the consul is his patron, he is a guest and no citizen, a fact says so. **Refused:** if his own power is at peace with the one he came to, he is given up, held (`captured`, the machinery of a man in another's hands) and the home court has the case against him (`bringTheCase`, a capital one); if it is at war with it, he is left without protection (`unprotected`: no patron, no guest-right, any magistrate may send him off). A power with no council (a king, a chief) receives him at once, as before. Test: `the-senate-decrees-a-refuge`.
2. **A hired band is its own people's** (`sim/hired-bands.ts`, called by `service_contract_open` when it raises a `company`). The playtest's hired Gaulish company was written "Legio II (hastati, principes, triarii)": the company was a row of men under the employer's power, and `formArmies` drew it up by the employer's establishment, whose default body took any infantry. (Reproduced in a test world through `service_contract_open` with `company`: the Roman establishment's `legion` body.) The band is now drawn up on the spot, one formation of its own, "the Gallic band of Brennus", under its captain, with the pay he was hired at (the contract's obligation and an account of its own), the unit structure the scenario's own establishments keep for the people (Carthage's Gallic, Iberian, Numidian, Balearic and Greek contingents; Syracuse's mercenary band for the Campanians), drilled and seasoned as a hired company is (`SOURCE_QUALITY.mercenary`), and the arms and the practice of its people for a doctrine of its own (`origin: "practice"`, `forceId` the band): the Gauls charge and do not hold (frontal weight up, steadiness down), the Iberians are at home in rough ground, the Numidians pursue, the Balearic slingers screen, the Campanians fight as the Samnites did, the Greeks stand in the phalanx. The people is read from the company's label, its duties, the captain's name and his culture; a company of no people the table knows is left to `formArmies` as it was. No scenario data is needed (`scenario-notes-defection.md`). Test: `a-hired-band-keeps-its-own-people`.
3. **What a defection does to a man's bargains and his bands** (`sim/defection-consequences.ts`, from `receiveDefector`). Every service contract the power he left let him is **broken** that day (`endContract`), so its pay stops (it went on to a deserter before). A hired band he led **follows him** or **falls back** to the power that hired it, by a hash of the man and the band: 35 in a hundred if it is paid to date, 25 more for each period unpaid, never over 90 (they follow the man who speaks for their pay: Polybius 1.66-69). A band that follows takes his command, goes under the new power and its pay is reckoned anew there; **the chest it takes is changed from one coinage to the other** at the money-changers' rate (`changedCoin`, `conversionBps`; `docs/economy.md`, "Coinages"), the cut leaving the books by the `abroad` door, so the money audit stays green. The regular armies he led go to the power's own officers (`handOverForcesOf`), as before. A man who **comes back** (`hasDeserted`: the infamia the home power keeps for life) is judged by it: Carthage's Hundred and Four try him for his life (a capital case laid before the court; he is held, `captured`, and the foreigner's mark lifted), a king has him put in chains; the same case is brought against a man the Senate gave up. His estate and family were dealt with the day he left ("in absentia", as built in batch 3). Tests: `a-defector-leaves-his-bargains-behind`.

Not done: a band taken on by its new power at a fresh wage is left to the new power's own officers and the player's order (no rule hires it); the people table lists six peoples (Cretans and Aetolians are read as Greeks, Ligurians and Galatians as Gauls; a company of Thracians or Illyrians, or of no people named, is left to `formArmies` as before); a deserter's estate abroad (holdings outside the home power) is not seized.
