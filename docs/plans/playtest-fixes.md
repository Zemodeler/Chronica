# Playtest fixes

Five hand playtests (`eval-out/playtest-*/NOTES.md`) found what follows. Each batch appends its own heading.

## Playtest fixes (batch 1): stability, orders and the ledger

1. **A local id built from an entity id.** `child:rome-kin-3ualq2:21` and `titus-vettius:comrade:4` failed the local-id rule and killed the whole burst. `sim/safe-local-id.ts` (`safeLocalId`) mends any id (lower case, other characters to underscores, cut to sixty with a hash of the whole) and leaves a valid one alone; every `localId:` template in the sim goes through it. `villainy.ts` vendettas, defamers and rivals are grown (`ADULT_YEARS`, fifteen) and living men only.
2. **The silent freeze.** A four-hundred-character promise went into a two-hundred-character label, and every tick after it was set aside without a word. `sim/clip-overlong.ts` cuts any string that is too long for its field (found from the schema's own `too_big` issues) in the tick, in the held bookkeeping steps and in a batch of acts; the producers in `promises.ts` and `commitments.ts` clip at source. A tick that still will not load stops the burst with stop reason `tick_failed`, puts the clock back to the last sound hop, logs the failing path to the console and tells the player.
3. **The engine does not skim for the player.** `keepDepartments` takes an `exempt` set (the player); monthly diversion skips his hands. He may take from the chest only by an order he writes, and an audit finds that as any other.
4. **A man with no charge builds nothing for the state.** The build template and the competence gate refuse a work paid from the public chest by a man who holds no seat and heads no power, in period words.
5. **The ledger tells what the world shows.** `OrderPart.actsDone` counts carried acts that are their own evidence (`DONE_ON_APPLICATION_OPS`: a purse debited, a holding made, a loan opened, men raised); where all are, the part is done, not "ordered; no sign of it yet". "Ordered, no sign" lapses after sixty days and is listed only for the latest order. A motion is always told (carried with the count, failed, or lapsed). "Nothing came of it that anyone could see" is not said of an order that was carried out. A speak-for order is not "raised" by a force that merely shares a name. A retried orchestrator part replaces its first answer (acts, facts, delegations) instead of standing beside it; a part refused with a reason, or told to stay as he is, is not asked for again; the same order is not handed to the same man twice; one usurpation is one answerable entry.
6. **Refusals give their reason.** The list of what is under way shows the engine's reason for a refused candidacy; a "propose" order refused by the army's indifference is told truly; a man refused by one he asked has that man's reason.
7. **Dispatches** go only to the man who holds the command above the sender (the army's controller) or heads the power; the books are reported to a magistrate who does not keep them; a doubled "writes:" is stripped; numbers are said as a man would (`menInWords`).
8. **A quaestor, a paymaster or any man of the supply** may say "find supply for the consul's army" (`isSupplyHand`, `forcesUnder(..., { forSupply: true })`); he does it himself.
9. **Declared people are made with the player** (`shared/characters/declared-kin.ts`: wife, son, patron, friend, with family links; the web's confirmation finds them there by name), re-enlistment keeps the service record, and a name another person bears gets "the Younger" and so on.
10. **The fact flood.** `isFarRoutine`/`ROUTINE_NEWS_KINDS` in `shared/world/facts.ts`: the calendar of a power that is not the observer's (terms, elections, harvests, census) is not known to him unless he found it out himself. Rome's own calendar stays.
11. **Stale text.** The seed line about a man's purse follows the purse; a soldier of the line with no age declared is seventeen to twenty-five, not thirty-five.
12. **A carried motion that begins nothing** says it is a resolution of the Senate that no one is charged with. Turning the carried text into an act by rule (a template for "that a depot be built") is not done: see open decisions.

Open decisions: item 12's by-rule execution; the orchestrator retry no longer asks again for a refusal the model gave with a reason (`orchestrate-split.test.ts` follows); whether the player should see the far calendar in a single batched line.

## Playtest fixes (batch 2): model calls, armies and the war

A. **Model calls** (the user's rule: a call is for a plot or a plan of a person near the player; far people never get one; about six a turn). Measured on the opening with a model that answers everybody "nothing to do", four seasons of ninety days: nine to ten calls a turn before (twenty-two asks of ten men, a far ruler asked five times in a season, an orchestrate call and two census rule-writes on a first turn with no order given), two to seven after, 4.5 on average (`a-turn-spends-few-calls`).
   1. A man of the cast's wider-world seats (`world`, `open`) who is far from the player and not in his circle is asked once and rests 45 days (21 if he is near), unless an order or a letter of the player's is with him (`sim/far-cast-rests.ts`, kept across turns in `cognitionReviews`, 45 days; `far-cast-men-rest-between-asks`). While he rests the rules play him (`playedByModel` leaves him out), so Carthage does not stand still for forty-five days. A round asks three at most (one call); those owed a turn or with a step falling due come before the rotation; a turn's cap is 4 calls and one for every twelve days, twelve at the most. `SimulationBudget.farRestDays` / `nearWorldRestDays` / `maxAskedPerRound` let a test turn the rests off (`plans.test.ts` does, for Hieron's plan).
   2. A man owed an answer is asked again only when what he owes changes (`hasSomethingNew`, `cognitionMemory.changed`, as before); the three-to-a-round cap and the rest stop it being every round.
   3. The rule-writer is not asked for an arrangement whose owner is far from the player's concerns and which his order did not set going (`anOwnerIsNear`): the Boii's census stands as the plain arrangement. The order's words are "what was said" only for an arrangement the order set going.
   4. The orchestrator is not shown stirrings of the world outside the player's reach (`aSeedIsWithinReach`: his realm, the ground near his places, his circle, his own person).
   5. A turn with no order costs no orchestrator call (the harness showed one on every such turn: the quiet answer was given only to a "continuation"); `a-wait-needs-no-orchestrator`.
B. **The Roman army did not march.** The rules stood back from every army a played man commands, and the played consul, asked, did nothing; the "orders" the rules had written in his name stood as a played head's do. Now an order written by the rules carries `byRule` and `forCommanderId`: they carry it out for a played general, look at it again like any rule-made order (the season, the bread, the road), and write it afresh in a new commander's name; the theatre's commander is part of the plan's allotment. An order the army cannot carry out (no road, no ships) is cancelled with its reason and the army keeps its quarters. A hungry town is not wintered in. On the Roman army of the legionary run: marches in the first month, Rhegium besieged and taken within five. `a-consular-army-marches-within-a-month`, `a-new-consul-takes-up-the-plan`, `an-impossible-order-is-replaced`.
C. **The war with Carthage.** Carthage holds western Sicily in the opening (an army of 3,200 at Lilybaeum, garrisons at Panormus and Agrigentum, three officers, pay; `db/punic-wars-sicily.ts`, `docs/plans/scenario-notes-batch2.md` for the sources and the doubts). A Senate refusal of a war on an appeal goes to the people within sixty days and, carried, the consul makes the war (`sim/the-appeal-goes-to-the-people.ts`); a hard-pressed people writes for help once in three hundred days; a request for peace or a truce has a term of sixty days (and is refused by silence at it). A legate's or a proconsul's proclamation of war or peace is refused with the reason, and his order to the same end is moved in the Senate for him, as before. `messana-calls-and-rome-answers`, `a-truce-request-is-answered`, `carthage-holds-western-sicily`.
D. **Rome at sea.** A rule-led power at war with a sea power, with no fleet, moves a fleet of 120 hulls by decree; the project is begun the day the Senate carries it and paid in three stages out of the chest; the Senate leans to it by the war's need where the enemy holds the sea (`sim/the-sea-is-lost.ts`; the war sanity saw it refused at every asking without that); the corvus is moved the same way once there are ships, and only by a power whose enemy has the better seamen, as a way of war priced by the existing reform machinery; the player's "build a fleet" is a template (`sim/a-fleet-by-decree.ts`, `order-templates.ts`). `rome-builds-a-fleet-by-decree`, `the-corvus-makes-boarding`.
E. **Opening forces.** The consular army is two legions and two alae (17,720, was 8,860), the legionaries' pay doubled with it; the tests struck on the smaller army take it back to that size (`sim/the-opening-as-it-was.ts`). A polity that ends now writes a line for its chest (`a-polity-that-ends-leaves-a-line-for-its-chest`): the money audit found the Bruttians' treasury moving to Rome's unexplained once the army marched into their country.

Open decisions: the famine of a taken city (Rhegium's food stays near 1,700 in ten thousand for a year after the siege, a thousand dead every two months, the hub's grain price at its cap; not the army's doing, and not fixed here: a task is raised for the economy); whether a far ruler's plan step due should wake him inside his rest; Agrigentum's garrison before 262; whether a legionary's order to a legate to "find ships" should take the template's path; the Carthage-Rome war arises by rule only if the Mamertines are pressed and ask (see the war sanity's `--free`).

## Playtest fixes (batch 3): elections, the economy, the merchant and the defector

1. **Elections keep the cursus.** `shared/characters/cursus.ts` (`cursusRefusal`, read by `admissionRefusal`, so by the election, the candidacy and the note of who could stand): one magistracy at a time (a man sitting in one he was chosen to within the last two months, or named to follow in one, is not admitted to a second the same day; the quaestors' seats no longer stand vacant because the praetor and the quaestor were the same man), the ladder of the custom of the age (the *lex Villia annalis* is of 180 BCE, later than 270: quaestor 1, aedile and plebeian aedile 2, praetor 3, consul 4; a man does not step back to a lesser rung or hold two of one; a year's rest after an office that is over; the consulship for a man who has been aedile or praetor, and at most two rungs up at a time; ages of 27, 31, 34 and 38, ten years' service from seventeen being Polybius VI.19; a man with no magistracy on record in the first years of a world is taken to have had the career his years imply; a tenure written at day 0 is a career before the world). It applies where the office asks for standing (a seat whose requirements were set aside, as the count's tests do, asks only after infamy). Tests: `one-man-one-office-a-day`, `the-cursus-keeps-its-order`.
2. **A conviction brands a man.** `sentenceByOutcome` (`trials.ts`) now also clears a magistracy the man was chosen for and not yet in (the condemned consul who "stayed consul" was a designate), writes an *infamia* record (ten years; for life if exiled) that shuts the magistracies, the military tribunate and the Senate, and lays the censors' mark (`nota`) so that he is not enrolled again until the next lectio lifts it. Test: `a-condemned-man-cannot-stand`. A man who goes over to another power is infamous for life in the one he left.
3. **"Succession disputed"** is not said of an election: `seizeThrones` and the contested-succession plot skip a seat that has a term (up to three years) or a college (`villainy.ts`, `isAnOrdinaryTerm`). **A general is not a suffete**: `INCOMPATIBLE_OFFICES` keeps a serving strategos of Carthage out of the suffetes' election (Aristotle, *Politics* 1272b-1273b).
4. **The player is told an election is called**, by name and with the words, when he may stand (`election_open_to_you`, `elections.ts`). Test: `the-player-is-told-an-election-is-called`.
5. **A state's loan is a negotiated act** (`sim/lending.ts`, `loanRefusal`, called by `loan_open`). A private man who lends to a state's chest needs the treasury's keeper to take it, by rule: the rate within what its credit bears (a centesima, one in a hundred a month, in peace; two at war), the sum within two months of its takings (not under 5,000), the term he wrote honoured (`termPeriodsOf`: "in 12 instalments"), a state in default lent to by nobody; a refused loan says why ("The Senate will not borrow at 100 in the hundred a month"). No loan between men is dearer than ten in the hundred a month. Fiscal officers' own loans, a standing offer taken, and loans from abroad are as they were. The merchant's 4,500 at "100% a month" is now refused (it paid back about 49,000). Test: `a-private-man-cannot-lend-the-state-into-the-ground`.
6. **Credit is told before it is taken.** `payOrBorrow` refuses a purchase of the player's that needs the seller's credit unless his own order said so (`CREDIT_WORDS`: "on credit", "take the credit", "borrow", "a loan"), and says what it is: "needs credit: 25% down (X), Y owed to the seller at 1% a month over 24 months. Say "on credit" in your order to take it". Test: `credit-is-told-before-it-is-taken`.
7. **A default has a price** (`priceOfDefault`, in the month's `settleDebts`): the debtor's purse goes to the lender (the pledge was always forfeit), his standing falls, he is infamous for five years (Rome after the *lex Poetelia Papiria* of 326 BCE chains nobody for debt: his goods are sold and he is struck infamous; Carthage fines him, the Greek cities strike him of his rights), and no lender deals with him (`defaultedUntil`); a state that defaults is lent to by nobody for five years. A fact says it. Test: `a-default-has-a-price`.
8. **Balance.** Estates pay 6 in the hundred a year of what they cost (land, `ESTATE_LAND_PRICE_MONTHS = 200`, the shares a tenth of what they were, the price unchanged), a land venture 17 (`VENTURE_TRADE_PRICE_MONTHS = 70`); a cargo makes 5 to 24 in the hundred a voyage before the crew, the customs and the changers (base 12), a ship takes at least two months to go and come back (`LEAST_ROUND_TRIP_DAYS`), the chance of a loss is 1.2 in a hundred for a fortnight at sea; a man's own cargo bears the crew and the customs as a house's does, and can be lost. A route is shut by the enemy's men at its ends (a third), beside it (two thirds), else a tenth (`trade-flows.ts`, `warOpenBps`; the hubs' flows keep their old reading). A firm needs 8,500 to sail and is refused below it with the number (`HOUSE_SAILING_FLOOR`; the reserve a house keeps was 12,000, it is 6,000). A declared merchant begins with 25,000 at the least and a senator with 60,000 (`db/punic-wars-merchant-start.ts`). `scripts/merchant-sanity.mts` gives the yields (below). The old figures: 61 for an estate, 80 for a venture, a firm 12,000 to 88,000 in five years (49 a year). Tests: `an-estate-pays-what-land-paid`, `a-firm-needs-its-floor`, `a-small-war-does-not-close-every-route`.
9. **The merchant is told.** "Your cargo, worth X, sailed from A for B" (known to him), "Your cargo landed at B: ... sold for Z, n% over; so much to the crew, so much in customs to P, so much to the money-changers", "lost at sea with the ship", and `venture_close`: "closing a venture pays nothing back". Test: `the-merchant-is-told-his-cargo-sailed`.
10. **Rome has Ostia** (`db/punic-wars-ostia.ts`): a port in Rome's own province (the castrum of about 349-338 BCE, medium confidence; the Ancus Marcius tradition is legend), so a venture from Rome is by sea. Hubs stay between forty and eighty. Test: `rome-has-ostia`. `docs/plans/scenario-notes-batch3.md` holds the version note.
11. **A firm may bid for the state's contracts** (`sim/bids.ts`, `trade-templates.ts`): "bid for the army's bread", "take the contract for the temple", "tender for the road at 9 percent" put in a bid of the firm he heads (a man with no firm is told to found one); the army's victualling (`army-victualling.ts`) and the censors' works (`keeping-the-state.ts`) go to the best bid (lowest margin, the reliable and the well-capitalised first) when it beats the usual margin, else to the lot as before; the winner and the beaten are told. Not done: farming the tribute (the Roman farming of Sicily's tithe begins after 241 with the lex Hieronica kept; Syracuse's collectors are the engine's own). Test: `a-firm-wins-the-army-bread`.
12. **A defector is a guest** (`sim/defection.ts`, called by `character_state_set` when a man's power changes; the order "seek refuge with Rome" is read by `refugeClause`): the ruler who receives him decides by rule (a ruler who thinks too ill of him turns him away), he is the ruler's client (`patron`), marked a `foreigner` and a `deserter` (the offices' rules and `cursusRefusal` read it: no tribe, no vote, no magistracy; `grantCitizenship` is the law of the people), infamous for life in the power he left, which acts by its own custom: Carthage tries him in his absence, takes his estate and holds his family as hostages; the companies he led fall back to the power's officers (or follow him where the order carried them over). Tests: `a-defector-is-a-guest-not-a-citizen`, `carthage-judges-its-deserter`.
13. **A senator does not stand in the ranks** (`force_membership_set` enlist refuses a man who sits in the council, and says he goes as a military tribune or a legate).

Open decisions: the scenario's authored estates (the Sabine farm, 1,200 a month, sixty in the hundred a year of a price nobody wrote) are ten times what land bought in play pays now, and want rescaling with the version bump; the mercenary company a contract raises has one kind of troops and no formations (the "Legio II (hastati, principes, triarii)" band was not reproduced: not done); the tribute is not farmed by a private bid; the receiving Senate's decree for a defector is not put as a motion (the consul's protection stands at once); `loanOffersFor` still values an estate at twenty months of yield, a tenth of what a bought one cost.
