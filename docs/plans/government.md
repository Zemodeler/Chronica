# Government: who may do what, per culture, around 270-264 BCE

Research for the government layer (see `docs/plans/constitutions.md` and
`docs/plans/departments.md`). Scope is 270-264 BCE, the opening of the Punic
Wars scenario in `packages/db/src/punic-wars-scenario.ts`. Later developments
are named only to be kept out. Confidence is marked **H**igh, **M**edium or
**L**ow. Where a claim rests on general scholarship rather than a page read
for this note, it is marked "(background)".

A caution that runs through everything: Polybius 6 describes Rome as it was
after 216 BCE, and Aristotle's Carthage is of the 330s. Neither is a snapshot of
270. The tables give the best reading for 270 and say where it is borrowed.

## Rome

Sources: Polybius 6, Lintott, Livy. The Republic in 270 was already the
"balanced" one: the lex Hortensia of 287 made plebiscites binding on all
(background), the plebs held half of the pontiffs' and augurs' places after the
lex Ogulnia (300), and the consulship was open to plebeians (367/342).

Key mechanisms:

- **Taxes and tribute.** Citizens paid the tributum only in emergencies, as a levy on the census, and it was repaid in part after wars (M, background). Revenue came from rents (vectigalia) of public land, mines, harbours and from plunder and indemnities. The censors let the collection of these revenues to companies of publicani in the five-yearly contracts (Pol. 6.17). The big provincial tithes belong to later conquests (Sicily from 241 and above all 210s on): keep them out of 270.
- **Coinage.** The Senate and magistrates with imperium issued; the mint worked at or near the temple of Juno Moneta; the tresviri monetales are probably later than their traditional 289 (source 6) (M-L). In 270 a consul or the Senate orders a striking: no separate mint office.
- **Spending.** The aerarium is the Senate's: no money without a decree, except what the consuls spend on the army (Pol. 6.12-13) (H). Quaestors keep the books.
- **Loans and debt.** No public debt in the modern sense: the state borrowed from citizens in need (the loans of 210 are later), and private debt was a tribune's grievance (nexum abolished 326) (background, M).
- **Grain and markets.** The aediles supervise the markets and the price of grain in the city; foreign grain buying and gifts of grain are the Senate's (M). A standing "annona" is later; keep the word out.
- **Customs.** Harbour dues (portoria) exist; they were let by the censors to publicani (M-L for the date and rate). Treaties may close or open a port (see Carthage).
- **Pacts, war, peace.** The Senate negotiates (embassies, 6.13) and the people ratify; war is voted by the people on the consuls' or Senate's motion; peace likewise (Pol. 6.14). Fetials make the declaration (background).
- **Levy.** The consuls hold the dilectus; allies send contingents per the formula (below).
- **Appointments.** Elected by the people; the dictator by a consul; the censors enroll the Senate; priests co-opt.
- **Justice and accountability.** No rendering of accounts at the close of an annual magistracy in 270; checks are tribunes' veto, prosecution (before the people, by tribunes or aediles) and the censors' nota. A standing extortion court (repetundae) is of 149; keep it out.
- **Terms.** One year; consuls iterate after a gap; prorogation is the Senate's, from 327 (source 7).

## Carthage

Aristotle (2.11) and Polybius 6.51 give the shape; the details of the 270s
are inferred (background; Warmington, Lancel, Huss).

Key mechanisms:

- **Tribute and taxes.** Carthage took tribute in kind and money from the Libyan subjects, harsh enough to feed revolt in 241; it taxed trade and levied a property tax in need (background, Polybius 1.72). The Council decided rates; collectors were its officers. Flag the details L.
- **Coinage.** The state issued silver and gold in Sicily and Spain for army pay; the Council authorised, generals' staffs issued in the field (background, M-L).
- **Spending.** The elders control the treasury; a general draws on it by their grant. Aristotle's complaint that offices go to the wealthy implies recouping by corruption (H).
- **Customs and ports.** The state kept foreign traders to named ports: the treaties of 509 and 348 have Romans trade only in Carthage, Libya and Sardinia and western Sicily under the state's guarantee of payment, and bar Roman ships from sailing past the Fair Promontory (sources 9) (H on the treaties' text, M on the reading). The treaty of 279, made against Pyrrhus, is a defensive alliance (M). Philinus' treaty of 306 (barring Romans from Sicily) is denied by Polybius (L; see disputes).
- **Pacts, war, peace.** The Council decides; when it splits, the people vote (Ar. 2.11). Armies are mostly mercenary, hired by the state; citizens serve in a small sacred band and navy.
- **Appointments.** By election, by wealth and merit; one man may hold several offices (Aristotle disapproves; H).
- **Accountability.** The Hundred and Four judge men after office (H that a body of this kind existed, M on its exact power).

## Syracuse under Hiero II

| Charge | Holder and term | Alone | With a colleague | Needs the body | Accountable to |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| King (basileus) | Hiero, general by the army's and citizens' choice about 275, called king soon after (the date 270 or 265 is disputed, M); a co-ruler Gelon from 241 (later) | Taxes and the grain tithe, the royal mint, the fleet and army, the treaties, appointments, justice | None; kinsmen (Gelon, Hieronymus) are later | Formal assembly: kept in form (Hiero "ruled within the law", source 4) | His own interest and the city; no formal check |
| Friends (philoi) | A small circle of advisers around the king | Advise; the king decides | -- | -- | The king |
| Strategoi | Several; named by the king, one year for civic ones | Command garrisons and levies; town governors | A board | Orders from the king | The king |
| Tithe collectors (decumani) | Private bidders for each district | Collect a tenth of the harvest under the law of Hiero | Contract per district | The king's agents or the city's | Penalised for taking more than a tenth (source 5) |
| Assembly of Syracuse | Citizens | Voices approval, no initiative (M-L) | -- | -- | -- |

Key mechanisms:

- **Taxes.** A tenth of the grain, with the right to collect sold by auction each year, under a law that bound both the farmer and the collector (Cicero, source 5) (H for the law's existence as Rome later found it, M for 270 as most of the detail is Cicero's of 70 BCE).
- **Coinage.** Hiero's own royal issues, with his name and a diadem; mint under the king's control.
- **Treaties.** The king alone, e.g. with Rome in 263 (source 4); his friends give advice that does not bind.
- **Spending.** The king's chest: no distinction between king and state (M).
- **War and levy.** The king raises a citizen army and mercenaries; the Mamertine war was the occasion of his rise.
- **Justice.** The king and the old civic courts.
- **Succession.** Hereditary after him; Gelon associated 241 (H, later).

| Dioiketes (chief finance officer) | King's appointee, no fixed term | Run revenues and the royal accounts; set the rolls | -- | The king for new taxes | The king |

Key mechanisms: taxes in kind and money, with monopolies (oil, papyrus, salt
and others) run by the crown; a royal bank; coinage reserved to the king
(a closed monetary system on the Ptolemaic standard) (source 11, M-H). Grain
is stored in royal granaries and exported by the crown. Customs are the king's
revenue, with duties collected at ports by contractors (M). War and treaties
are the king's, with his friends' counsel. No assembly of citizens binds him
(H). Alexandria's citizen body has its own bodies but no sovereignty (M).

is hereditary with the risk of kin rivalry (H). Far less is certain about
fixed rules for provincial fiscal officers before Antiochus III (L-M).

- Carthage: suffete (sufet, "judge"), the Council of the Elders, the Hundred and Four, the people (the assembly), the accounts, the general, the priests of Baal Hammon, the treasury of the temple (M).
- Greek and royal: basileus (king), philoi (the king's friends), strategos (general), dioiketes (royal finance officer), oikonomos (steward), satrap, nome, ekklesia (assembly), boule (council), synodos, damiourgos, euthyna (the audit at the close of office), telonai (tax farmers), decumani (collectors of the tenth), the royal chest, the tithe.

## Disputed and thin evidence

Most important first.

1. **Polybius 6 is later than 270 (M).** It describes a Rome after the Second Punic War. The Senate's control of the treasury and of foreign affairs is probably older (the Senate was long the guiding body) but the full "mixed constitution" picture is Polybius' (Walbank, Lintott). Simplification: use Polybius' competences for 270, but leave out the standing courts and provincial governors.
2. **Carthaginian bodies (L-M).** The "kings" are suffetes; the numbers of the Elders (some say 300, some 30 as an inner body), the Hundred and Four's origin (Aristotle names "the hundred" and Justin the 104), who elected generals, and whether the Hundred and Four judged all magistrates or only generals (see source 3). Simplification: the Council decides; the people only when the suffetes and Council split (Aristotle); the 104 try generals and magistrates after office.
3. **Syracusan kingship and assembly (M-L).** Hiero was a general before he was king; the date of the title (270 vs 265) and whether a civic assembly kept real power are unclear (sources 4). Simplification: Hiero is king, takes decisions alone with his friends' counsel, and keeps the assembly as a form.
4. **Philinus' treaty of 306 (L).** Polybius denies it; most scholars side with Polybius. Treat the 509, 348 and 279 treaties as real and the 306 clause as rumour (source 9).
5. **Number of quaestors in 270 (L-M).** Four before c. 267, eight after (and the scenario's four). Tresviri monetales date (source 6).
6. **Rome's iteration rule (M).** The lex Genucia's date and content are doubtful; ten-year interval is from Livy's age of repeat consulships. 
7. **Ptolemaic and Seleucid fiscal detail (M-L).** The Revenue Laws are of c. 259; they are used for a state of 270 on the assumption of continuity.
8. **Macedonian assembly (L).** How far the army assembly could hold a king to account.
9. **Allied obligations (M).** The formula togatorum is of the 220s; for 270, treat the amounts as unknown, the obligation as real.
10. **Roman debt and grain (L-M).** A treasury loan from citizens and Senate buying of grain before the annona are thin before 216.

### Competence

An act is one of `tax_rate`, `customs`, `coinage`, `loans`, `grain`, `pacts`, `embargoes`, `levy`, `war`, `peace`, `appointment`, `spending`. For each, an
office stands one of four ways: **alone** (within his charge), **with a colleague** (collegial: either may act and the other may stop it: *par potestas*,
*intercessio*), **by the body** (laid before the chamber that holds that power; he may move it, and may not do it) or **forbidden**. A line may carry a figure
(`within`, in the act's own unit) and what lies beyond it (`beyond`): a rate past the customary 12 in a hundred is the chamber's.

It is derived, in this order, from the office's own `charge` (data a template or a scenario writes), then from the parts:

- the chamber that decides the act: one that binds (not advisory) and holds the power (`chamberHolds`), the one that names it first; failing one, the sovereign
  chamber of the constitution; failing that, nobody but the head of state;
- the head of state (`Constitution.rulerOfficeId`) holds every act alone where no chamber holds it (a king: his council gives its opinion and he need not obey it, which is H3);
  in a republic he stands **with his colleague, within custom** for the rate (12 in a hundred), the customs (15), loans (3 months of what the power takes in), bread (2 months),
  the levy and appointments, and **by the body** for war, peace, pacts, embargoes and the coin. A man set above every chamber (a dictator: `capital_set` among his orders) holds all alone;
- an officer's charge from the levers of the department he staffs (or, failing one, what his name says, `leversNamedBy`): the tax roll, the customs or the credit make a
  treasurer, who sets the rate (12) and the customs (10) and borrows (1 month) with his colleagues; grain or markets, bread (1 month); the foreign letters or the customs,
  pacts and embargoes (by the body); the levy or the supply (or `force_create` among his orders), the levy;
- spending: from the office's treasury reach. `spend_without_vote` is an allowance (two months of takings for a head, one for an officer, in a year, for each office, counted in
  `ActMark`); `propose_spending` alone is by the body; a department whose `gates` name a chamber for `spend` is by that chamber whatever else it has (the Treasury of Saturn paid out
  nothing the Senate had not voted);
- a seat in a council or a college and a priesthood hold no charge (the head of a temple state does); the tribune holds none, and **may stop** the levy, the purse, the tribute, the
  coin, bread and appointments (`Competence.mayStop`).

An abolished office is forbidden everything ("has been abolished"). Competence is read again from the parts each time (cached for a world object), so a reform, a coup or a vacated
seat changes it at once (`a-reform-changes-who-may-act.test.ts`).

**Chamber powers.** `ChamberPower` gains `coinage`, `treaties`, `finance` and `levy`. A chamber written before them (a list naming none of the four) holds each with the older power it
was part of (`POWER_IMPLIED_BY`: coinage and finance with taxes, treaties and levy with war), so Rome's Senate keeps the mint, the purse and the treaties; naming any of the four takes a
chamber out of the old bundle. `QuestionConcern` gains `coinage` (the blocs lean by what each wants of the silver in the coin: creditors and merchants against, debtors and the crown for); a
question's concerns map to the powers it needs (peace to treaties, the levy to levy, spending to finance, coinage to coinage: `senate.ts` `powersNeeded`), so a body that lacks the power
counts only an opinion (`isAdvisoryFor`).

### The gate

`apply-deltas.ts` asks `competenceGate` (after the authority check, before `nobodyListens`) about: `tax_policy_set` (rate and customs scopes), `coinage_debase`, `loan_open` (the state
borrowing), `money_transfer` from a power's chest (and `service_contract_open`, a hiring paid from it: the advance and the pay for the term), `public_benefaction` of kind dole (bread), and `agreement_open` / `agreement_close` of the war, peace, pact and embargo kinds. Only for an
act of the actor's own power, by a man who holds an office of it, in a power with a constitution on record. Not for what a vote has carried (`sanctionedDeltas`: the fiscal officers' own
decisions are sanctioned the same way, and are only recorded), nor the world's own business (`actsForTheWorld` apart from the order), nor a treaty an accepted letter made.

- **Alone** or **with a colleague**: done, and the office is the authority where no grant said so. A colleague of equal power who has interceded (an `intercession` arrangement, as a
  tribune makes) stops it, and so does one who thinks ill of him (`RIVAL_OPINION`).
- **Beyond his figure** or **by the body**: laid before the chamber that holds the power, as a war is (`layBeforeTheChamber` for treaties, a motion built here for a rate, a coin, a
  loan, a draw or bread), and done the day it carries (the order's held act, `sanctionedDeltas`). Only the player's order is laid for him; a man's own word is told that it is the
  chamber's, and nothing is opened.
- **Forbidden**: where a grant had said he might, refused; where it had not, the engine answers as it always did.
- **One decider to a season** (`ACT_WINDOW_DAYS`: the rate 90 days, the customs and the coin 60, a loan, bread and a pact 30): after one has settled an act, another man of equal or lower
  office may not settle it again until the season is out; a higher office may. What the chamber has decreed no magistrate alters in the season.
- Tribunes: `whose-to-give.ts` lets an intercession name the tribute and the coin (`tax`), loans and bread (`spending`), and lets a colleague, not only a tribune, make one.

Trade letters go to `chargeHolders(..., "pacts")`: the man whose office keeps the customs or its letters, else the head of state (`trade-desk.ts`); the verdict is his.

### The five charges

Levers `customs`, `mint`, `markets`, `credit`, `census` (`ECONOMIC_LEVERS`; the portfolio a ruler is counted as holding for his workload is still the twelve, so nothing is added to a
consul's load). Each is a multiplier near one, `1 + skillShare(skill, reach)`, by the skill of the **department's** men (`chargeSkill`) and **exactly one where nobody has been given the
charge**, so a world that sets nobody to them runs as it did.

| Charge | Reach | What moves | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| customs | 0.2 | the share of the duty on the month's trade that reaches the chest (smuggling and lax collectors lose up to a fifth) | `customs.ts` |
| mint | 0.15 / 0.3 | the gain from a coin struck lighter (and the cost of striking it better, divided by it); how much of a coin's war, arrears and high-price name sticks to its premium | `apply-deltas.ts` `coinage_debase`, `coinage.ts` |
| markets | 0.2 | how far a hub's price strays from the base price; and at a famine price (grain at 140% or more) up to 6 in a hundred of the shortfall a month is brought out of hoards, for a skill above fifty | `markets.ts` |
| credit | 0.15 | the interest a state is asked on a loan | `apply-deltas.ts` `loan_open` |
| census | 0.08 | what the tax roll can raise of the land (`bearable`) and the men a tribute of men counts | `material/taxation.ts` |

### Spending and the year's allowance

`spendingAllowance(world, officeId)`: months of `monthlyTakings` (the income sources paying into the power's chests, at their collection rate) times the office's `within`, less what the office
has drawn in the year (360 days from the first draw). A consul of Rome: 2 months (about a million drachmae). A quaestor: none (the Senate's gate). Past it, the draw is laid before the Senate.

### How it reads

(The opening of the Punic world: `scripts/_probe-competence.mts`.)

### Carthage's head

The scenario named `carthaginian-strategos` (the commander in Sicily) as Carthage's `headOfficeId`, so the constitution read him as head of state and the two suffetes as ordinary magistrates. The research says the
suffetes are the civil heads and the general is chosen for the term of a war and answers to the Hundred and Four on his return (Aristotle, Politics 2.11; Polybius 6.51). v55: `headOfficeId` is `carthaginian-suffete`;
the strategos is a general (the levy, the army's coin, the field chest; war forbidden, peace laid before the elders); the officers of the accounts and the Council read as in the table. What follows from it:
the men who rule Carthage (`readDepartments(...).rulers`) are the two suffetes, so the letters to Carthage and the peace it signs go to them (the peace table's speaker, `diplomaticAnswererOf`); Hanno is a general of
the Messana thread, and no longer a member of the standing cast, which has Carthage's voice from the rules. (Open decision: a +10 to a general in an open thread would put him back, at the cost of the rotation of

### The chambers, said outright

Rome's Senate lists `laws, war, taxes, constitution, judgment, coinage, finance, treaties, levy` (everything but the elections), the centuriate assembly `elections, laws, war, treaties, judgment`, the tribal `elections,
laws, judgment`; Carthage's Council `laws, war, taxes, constitution, coinage, finance, treaties, levy`. Nothing changes of what each chamber could decide (a chamber that named none of the four newer powers held
them by the old bundle), but each now says so by name, which is how `bodyFor` finds the chamber an act is laid before. `bodyFor` now takes the *first* power an act names before the next: an appointment is the people's
(`elections`) though the Senate also holds the laws. War stays with the Senate in the engine (Polybius has the people vote it on the Senate's motion: the people stand second, in the list, and a simplification the
doc names under thin evidence, item 1); the people ratify treaties (`treaties` is theirs too, after the Senate).

### A new tax is the Senate's

`gatedAct` weighs a `tax_policy_set` of a regime the power did not lay before (a land levy turned into a tithe, a tribute of men into coin) as a figure past every `within`: no magistrate lays a new tax, whatever the
rate, and a king, whose line has no figure, does. A rate within custom is still a magistrate's. The fiscal officers' shift of a famine year's levy to a tithe is the same act and asks the Senate (`a-magistrate-lays-no-new-tax.test.ts`).

### The foedus and the augurs

A power bound to a leader by foedus (`leaderOf`) has no war or peace of its own for any office of its constitution; the line reads "Rome makes the war and the peace of those bound to it by the foedus", and the
apply layer already held it to it (the ally's trade pacts stand: the research says they may trade). If it breaks with Rome the line is read from the parts again. An augur's office (a priesthood that vetoes) *may stop*
the naming of men to office, as an omen stops the assembly of the day; the gate does not weigh appointments, so it is a reading for the office view and nothing in the engine stops an election by it yet.

### The men at the books, through their competence (open decision 3 of H0)

`decideFiscal` weighs each thing the officer would do (the rate, the customs, a loan, the coin, a pact, the lifting of an embargo) against `mayAct` for him, at the figure he would do it at:

- **his own** (alone, or with a colleague): done, as before;
- **the chamber's**: a motion is opened before the chamber that holds the power (`political_procedure_open`, a council deliberation about the power, in the words "that the tribute of Rome be laid at 14 in a
  hundred", the same words the gate lays a player's order in), sponsored by a magistrate who may convene it (the quaestor does not convene the Senate; a consul puts it, and the account says so). Once: not while it waits
  on the vote, and not for 120 days after one failed (`MOTION_REST_DAYS`);
- **decreed**: a motion that carried is carried out the next month the officer looks, as far as the decree goes (a figure no higher than the one voted) and within 120 days;
- **none of his**: left. A quaestor of Rome has no say in the coin, so Rome never strikes it lighter by rule (the Senate did in the Second Punic War; that is a consul's to move).

What the chamber votes it votes (`holdVotes`: its blocs, its referral, the assembly's advice), so an AI's tribute can fail in the Council and go to the people, which is the research's reading of Carthage; a rejected motion
rests. The burst applies the officer's motions as the power's standing business (`sanctioned`); `StatecraftInput` gains an optional `offices` (the scenario's offices) so `mayAct` reads the offices written by hand.

### Appointments by rule

A ruler who is overloaded, whose chest is in debt, whose people are hungry or who has been struck at no longer waits for an order or for the model to think of it: in the month's review (`appointed-by-rule.ts`, before `fillAppointments`) he makes the charge his state lacks.
The `wantDepartments` ambitions (hand work on, a treasurer, a watch, a keeper of the grain) are still laid, as the record of the pressure, and are marked fulfilled when the charge is made; a man the model plays is not shown them (`management-talk.ts`).

- **Kings** make the charges a kingdom had, by culture: for a Greek court the dioiketes (`tax_roll`, `credit`), the sitophylax (grain), the epistates of the harbours (customs), the strategos of the host (`levy`, `supply`), the archiphylakites (watch), the epistates of the
  courts; for another court a treasurer of the realm, a keeper of the granaries, a warden of the harbours, a marshal of the host, a master of the watch, a chief judge. What presses comes first (debt, hunger, a plot struck, the workload of -9), then, a year apart and only for a king,
  the finance officer, the harbours (a kingdom with ports) and the host. The office is new, with one seat; the department forms in 30 days (60 for more than two levers or in a war); the ablest man of the power for its levers is named the day the post stands empty.
- **Republics, leagues and the rest** invent no magistrate: the Roman magistracies are elected and the Senate assigns the commands, and Carthage's suffetes carry an appointment to the Elders (`mayAct` on `appointment` reads "by the body" for both), so for them
  the rule makes no charge. Where the head may name alone or with a colleague, and the power already has an appointed post that sits in no department and whose name says a charge (an admiral, a treasurer), the work is given to that post.
- **Never the player's.** A power whose ruler is the player is left to him, and the engine never seats him. The model is not asked: the ambitions are hidden from its prompts and the acts are the officers'.
- **Bounds.** One charge a power in 90 days, five in all, four in a review across the world (the powers shuffled by month); one fact a charge. Deterministic (`stableHash` only), no model call.

Not done: the chambers do not vote a charge into being for a republic by rule (a Senate decree founding a department is still an order's); a charge made by rule is honorary, with no pay of its own; no officer is ever removed or replaced by rule except through the posts' existing
turnover (`fillAppointments` fills what stands empty).

## H2 as built

Phase H2: the men who hold a charge act, once a month, by rule. `sim/src/magistrates.ts` is a small registry (a charge and its routine); each routine reads the state of its charge, and the man who holds it acts inside his
competence through the ordinary deltas, so authority, the audits and the money audit apply. The fiscal officers (`fiscal-officers.ts`), the upkeep and the works (`keeping-the-state.ts`) and the houses
(`merchant-houses.ts`) keep their own routines where they stand: they were built before the registry and share its helpers (`weigh`, `readTheBooks`, `grainStress`, the log as memory) instead of being moved into it.

### The man

His worth is the skill of his lever with his temper (`qualityOf`); a poor man acts late (`35 + 0.65 x worth` in a hundred a month, more in an emergency). One who thinks ill of the head of state or is too proud refuses or
puts it off by the reckoning of an order refused (`defianceOf`). The disloyal drag the lever already (`SULK_*` in the department's skill). The greedy keep a share of what they hand out (`skimTheHand`: the same
`diversionShare`, at most 12 in a hundred, a transfer from the chest to his purse or his patron's, a `Diversion` row an audit can find: the burst takes it after the act). A man the player is, a power the player rules and a
man the model plays are left alone; a played ruler's books follow the played-ruler rule (`played-ruler-books.ts` now counts a magistrate's marks as the rules').

### The gate (the gaps closed)

`force_provision` (bought or sent), `project_create` (milestone costs paid from a power's chest) and `force_create` (not an outlaw band, not men taken from another army) are weighed in `competence-gate.ts`: the first two as
spending against the year's allowance (a man with no office of the power is not weighed), the last as the levy.

### News and the log

At most one fact a power a month, only for a power with something live in it; the dole and the games tell their own. The decision's `why` begins with its routine's name (`grain:`, `dilectus:`, `muster:`,
`supply:`, `justice:`, `festival:`, `census:`, `mint:`, `embassy:`, `games:`); the cooldowns are read off the log (`STATECRAFT_LOG_MAX` is 900 now), and a matter laid before a chamber is not logged as done (it begins `asked`).

### A sample month (Rome at war with Carthage; Rome hungry in one market of two; Carthage's chest low; day 180)

- Rome: Appius Claudius Russus (aedile) "grain: 414000 spent on bread (dear in 1 of 2 markets)"; Manius Curius Dentatus (censor) "census: the roll of Roman Republic taken, 1101300 counted"; Tiberius Coruncanius
  (pontifex maximus) the festival of the year; Marcus Atilius Regulus (quaestor) asks the Senate that the tribute be laid anew as a tithe.
- Carthage: Abdmelqart (officer of the accounts) raises the rate from 10 to 12; Hanno holds the levy, 3,368 men (the host fields 3,110 of 17,967).
- Syracuse: Hiero's alliance against Carthage (a king: his own); with his friends against a war, a headstrong Hiero pays 400 basis points of legitimacy.

### Cost

A month over the whole Punic world (170 powers, 513 seats): the magistrates' pass 27 ms with the board and the books warm (about 40 ms more when the board is cold, which the world's own pass pays anyway); the bodies' pass
16 ms with a hundred questions open; the whole statecraft pass with the fiscal officers and the magistrates 135 to 180 ms. A power's routine looks for its man only when its condition holds.

the dilectus as reinforcement of an existing army, a magistrate's accounts at the close of his office (H4).

## H3 as built

Phase H3: the bodies sit. The voting is the old machinery (`senate.ts`, `ballots.ts`, blocs, `supportPositions`, the gens' pull, the tribunes' intercession, `refersFailuresTo`, `overruleCost`); `sim/src/bodies.ts` is what
makes a body a body, and `statecraft.ts` is where a republic's war, peace and alliances become the chamber's.

- **Sittings and agenda.** A chamber puts at most five questions to a sitting if it is a council, three if its count only counsels, two for an assembly of the people or a court (the Hundred and Four judge two a sitting);
  what is due beyond it is put off a month, the oldest first; a chamber whose city is held by an enemy or besieged does not sit. A chamber holds at most ten questions open (`BACKLOG_MAX`): a magistrate who would lay an
  eleventh waits (`weigh` asks `bodyHasRoom`). Quorum and majority are each chamber's own, by `countVote`.
- **The tribunes.** On the day a question comes up, a tribune who has said nothing and whom nobody plays takes a side by what he is (`mindLean` of the question's concerns), whom he serves and whom he thinks ill of, and
  what the question asks of the plebs (taxes, the levy, war, debts, the coin, spending): `opposition` of 40 and over, and a roll, and he opposes; `holdVotes` then forbids it and tells it. In the tick, before the count.
- **The augurs.** A question for an assembly of the people (not a council) may be put off a month by an augur who sees bad omens (4 in a hundred, more against a man he thinks ill of and for his party), once for a question
  (`theAuspices`; the office's `mayStop` is `appointment`, the people's business).
- **War, peace and treaties.** For a republic whose ruler's competence is "by the body" (Rome's consul, Carthage's suffete), `passage` lays the war (or the peace, or an alliance) before the chamber that holds it as a motion
  ("that war be made on Carthage") and makes it the month after it carries (`weigh`'s decree); a failed motion rests 120 days; a power with no offices to read it from, a king and a dictator act as they did. A war the
  Senate carried is read to the assembly that holds the power of war and ratified: `RATIFICATION_BPS` (150) of legitimacy once (`ratifyTheWar`). The user's accepted simplification stands: war is the Senate's in the
  engine, the people's ratification a bonus.
- **The king's friends.** A king about to make war asks his friends in his mind (`consultTheFriends`: the council's count taken now): against, a heeding king (by `stubbornness`, 5 to 85 in a hundred from boldness,
  caution, the drive to rise and duty) lets it wait; a headstrong one goes on and pays 400 basis points of legitimacy (half where the overruling has become a custom). Counsel a council has given on a question already put is
  taken, bowed to or overruled by the ruler by rule (`decideCouncils`: a `political_procedure_resolve`; the ordinary `overruleCost` charges the overruling).
- **Carthage.** The Elders hold taxes, war, the coin, the purse, the treaties and the levy and send a rejected magistrate's question to the people; the Hundred and Four judge generals and, from H2, the cases the praetor brings.

### Not in H3

Command assignment and prorogation by rule (`command-tenure.ts` has the prorogation; the Senate does not assign provinces by rule), a real quorum of members away at war (the count's quorum is the blocs', and every bloc
sits), the people's own vote on war (ratification only).
